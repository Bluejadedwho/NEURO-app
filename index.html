NeuroSync Program Protocol

1. The Core Philosophy

This is not a strength workout; it is Neuromuscular Re-education. In AMN, the signal from the brain to the legs is "noisy" or degraded.

Our goal is to reduce that noise by removing gravity (Bed/Side-Lying) and slowing down. We are trying to teach your brain to find the specific muscle pathway again without using "helper" muscles (compensation).

2. The Golden Rules

  • Quality > Quantity: 10 perfect reps where you feel the right muscle are better than 50 sloppy ones.
  • Stop at Fatigue: As soon as your form breaks or your leg starts shaking uncontrollably, stop. That is your nervous system saying "I'm done listening."
  • No Momentum: Do not swing your leg. Lift it with control, hold it with control, lower it with control.

3. Progression Strategy

Start with the Bed-Based and Side-Lying sections. Only move to Functional Standing work when you feel confident and stable.

MODULE 1 – My Motor Pattern▼
Motor-drive dominant upper motor neuron pattern with preserved passive range and speed-sensitive control.

I have spinal adrenomyeloneuropathy (AMN) affecting the corticospinal tracts (the primary voluntary motor pathways from brain to spinal cord).

This results in:

  • Sustained clonus (repetitive reflex contractions)
  • Positive Babinski sign (upper motor neuron reflex)
  • Impaired voluntary motor drive
  • Speed-dependent movement breakdown
  • Co-contraction (simultaneous activation of opposing muscles)
  • Preserved passive range of motion

My phenotype is motor-drive dominant rather than severe spasticity dominant.

This means:
The pathway is impaired, not absent.
Slow movement improves performance.
Speed amplifies motor noise.

This is neurologically coherent and trainable.

MODULE 2 – Compensation & Pain▼
How neurological drive limitations lead to pelvic shear and buttock pain.

Reduced voluntary hip flexion and extension drive leads to:

Myelopathy
→ Reduced motor recruitment
→ Hip hiking
→ Trunk momentum substitution
→ Pelvic shear (micro-sliding forces at the sacroiliac joint)
→ Deep rotator overwork
→ Buttock pain
→ Sympathetic amplification during flares

Pelvic shear = small asymmetrical forces across the sacroiliac joint when stability is compromised.

The SI belt reduces shear by increasing compression.
Pain variability proves this system is modifiable.

MODULE 3 – Speed Rule▼
Slow is therapeutic.
Fast walking increases:
  • Velocity-dependent tone
  • Co-contraction
  • Hip hiking amplitude
  • Pelvic shear
  • Fatigue & Pain risk
Slow walking improves:
  • Motor planning
  • Signal-to-noise ratio
  • Symmetry
  • Stability

Momentum is compensation.
Precision is progress.

MODULE 4 – Flare Protocol▼
When pain is high, reduce shear and signal overload.
Do:
  • Wear SI belt
  • Use firm upright seating
  • Limit sitting blocks
  • Quad sets & Ankle pumps
  • Gentle glute activation
  • Slow side-lying knee flexion (pelvis stacked)
Avoid:
  • Clamshells
  • Side-lying hip abduction
  • Forcing standing hip extension
  • High-speed walking
  • Deep couch sitting
MODULE 5 – Build Phase▼
Train signal quality before load.
Begin with:
  • Supine glute activation
  • Small bridges
  • Prone assisted hip extension
  • Band feedback drills
  • Slow, controlled reps
Progress gradually:
  • Light mini-bike
  • Shallow squats
  • Controlled posterior chain work

Avoid lateral dominance early.
Quality > quantity.
Precision > power.

MODULE 6 – Seating Guidelines▼
Neutral pelvis reduces shear.
Ideal seating:
  • Firm base
  • Hips slightly higher than knees
  • Neutral pelvis
  • Easy stand-up position
Avoid:
  • Deep, soft couches
  • Posterior pelvic tilt collapse
  • Long static sitting

Stability > softness.

MODULE 7 – Quick Reference▼
Quick rules for daily navigation.
  • Slow is therapeutic.
  • Signal before load.
  • If speed increases compensation, reduce speed.
  • If trunk rocks forward/back, reset.
  • Wear belt during high-demand periods.
  • Pain is load-sensitive, not purely structural.
  • This phenotype is trainable.

Straight Leg Raises ❓

Protocol: Lie completely flat on your back. Keep your knee locked straight (active quad). Lift the leg slowly to about 45 degrees. Hold for 1 second, then lower slowly.

Quad Sets ❓

Protocol: Place a small rolled towel under your knee if needed. Press the back of your knee firmly down into the bed to engage the thigh muscle. Hold for 5 full seconds.

Ankle Pumps ❓

Protocol: Pull your toes up toward your nose (dorsiflexion), then point them down. Then do slow circles.

Knee Flexion ❓

Protocol: Lie on your side. Slowly slide your heel up towards your buttock. Focus on the hamstring.

Hip Lift (Abduction) ❓

Protocol: Lie on your side. Lift the top leg straight up. Keep hips "stacked".

Supported Knee Press-Down ❓

Protocol: Lie on your side with knees bent and hips stacked. Place a pillow or folded towel between your knees if that feels better. Instead of lifting the top knee yourself, gently let the top knee separate only as much as it naturally can or with a little setup help, then press the top knee back down slowly with control. Count one rep each time you press down and return to the start. This is a modified replacement for clamshells when active lifting is limited.
